MNA1010 · Airline business models and strategy

AirStrategist

You are the new CEO of a start-up carrier based at Geneva (GVA) — an airport where a network carrier and Europe's largest low-cost base both make money, by doing completely different things. Choose a business model, pick which routes to fly, set the aircraft, cabin layout, fares by cabin and frequency, and run it for eight quarters (two years). Every later decision either reinforces the model you chose or quietly contradicts it. The board is watching for fit, not for any single clever move. Demand, competitor fares, aircraft and airport costs are drawn from the real MNA1010 dataset.

1 · Understand the two models first

Read this before you choose. The simulator rewards a strategy that hangs together, not a lucky quarter. Dotted-underlined terms anywhere in the simulator show a definition when you hover or tap them; the full glossary is at the bottom of the page.

2 · Choose your business model

About the seed. The seed fixes everything random in the two years ahead — when shocks land, how fuel drifts, how competitors move their fares. It is not case-sensitive. Everyone using the same seed faces an identical scenario, so use the seed your lecturer gives you: it makes your result comparable with every other group and lets the class be debriefed against one story. Change the seed only if you are told to, or if you are practising on your own. You will not be told what is coming; a real CEO isn't either.

Start position: €30M cash, four possible routes from Geneva — London Heathrow, Paris (CDG), Amsterdam, Alicante — nine aircraft types on lease, three cabins (First / Business / Economy). Fuel and environmental charges drift and rise. Something will go wrong.
